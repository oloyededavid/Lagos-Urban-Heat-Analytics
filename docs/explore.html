The study in plain terms

  • The place. Lagos State's 20 Local Government Areas, from the crowded mainland core to the greener east and west.
  • The question. Which areas get hottest in the dry season, where does that heat meet the most people, and what goes with cooler places?
  • How. Free satellite data: four decades of ground surface temperature (1985–2026), plus vegetation, built-up density and population for the most recent dry seasons.

      Methods, audit & limitations

      How this was measured

      1. Areas

      The 20 Local Government Areas of Lagos State from FAO GAUL 2015 (level 2). The simplified boundaries contain stray line fragments; these are removed when the export is installed, so contiguity-based statistics work. Each LGA's land area excludes open water (Sentinel-2 MNDWI > 0), and population density is people per km² of land.

      2. Surface temperature

      Landsat 8 and 9 Collection 2 Level-2 surface temperature (ST_B10 × 0.00341802 + 149 K), cloud, shadow and dilated-cloud masked, over land only, as the median of all dry-season scenes (December–March of 2022/23 and 2023/24). This is the temperature of the ground and roofs at the morning overpass (about 10:30), not the air temperature people feel. MODIS Terra 8-day daytime LST (1 km) over the same period is an independent cross-check.

      3. Vegetation, built-up density and terrain

      Sentinel-2 surface reflectance, cloud-masked by the Scene Classification Layer, as the dry-season median: NDVI = (B8 − B4)/(B8 + B4) and NDBI = (B11 − B8)/(B11 + B8), over land only. Elevation and slope from the Copernicus GLO-30 DEM.

      4. Hot spots and clustering

      Global Moran's I with Queen contiguity and k-nearest-neighbour weights (k = 3, 5, 8), with 999 permutations. Local Getis-Ord Gi* on k-nearest neighbours, two-sided p-values, with Benjamini–Hochberg false discovery rate control at 0.05. An LGA is k-stable if its z-score keeps the same sign and |z| ≥ 1 for all three k.

      5. Heat exposure index

      Min–max scaled surface temperature × min–max scaled log(1 + population density), from 0 (cool and sparse) to 1 (hottest and densest). A rank-based version is computed as a robustness check.

      6. What explains the heat

      Ordinary least squares regressions of surface temperature on NDVI, on NDBI, and on both, with classical 95% confidence intervals, a leave-one-out check and Moran's I of the residuals. NDVI and NDBI are almost perfectly inversely related across the 20 LGAs, so their separate effects cannot be told apart reliably.

      7. Four decades of change

      Every Landsat 4, 5, 7, 8 and 9 scene of every dry season from 1985 to 2026 (from 2014, Landsat 8 and 9 only). Frames of the same orbit on the same day are joined into one pass. In each pass, each LGA's surface urban heat island is its mean land temperature minus the mean of a fixed rural reference: land within 20 km of Lagos State that the EU Global Human Settlement Layer shows as essentially unbuilt in 2020. Because city and countryside are measured in the same pass, the day's weather, haze and sensor largely cancel. Passes where more than 5% of Lagos land reads over 3 °C colder than the countryside are rejected as cloud or fog the cloud mask missed; a pass must also keep at least 3,000 clear reference pixels (about 100 km²), and an LGA counts in a pass only if half of its land is clear. Each period's value is the median across passes, with a bootstrap 95% interval; trends are Theil–Sen slopes across dry seasons with a Mann–Kendall test, corrected for multiple testing across the LGAs with enough seasons to test. Built-up change comes from GHSL (1995 to 2020). Every pass considered is listed in lagos_change_scenes.csv.

      8. Limitations

      Twenty large areas give little statistical power and hide variation inside each LGA (the ecological fallacy and the modifiable areal unit problem); the heat-island maps show the detail inside them. Results are associations, not causes or forecasts. Only the dry season is covered. Population is WorldPop 2020 and boundaries date from 2015. For the change analysis: the Landsat archive for West Africa has a gap from 1992 to 1999 and few clear scenes in the late 1980s, so the first period has wide intervals; Landsat 5 and 7 passed over earlier in the morning than Landsat 8 and 9; Landsat 7 has striping after May 2003; the rural reference itself may have changed (farming, forest loss), which would make the heat island look smaller, not larger; and GHSL 2020 stands in for the latest period's built-up area.
      About this project

      How to use this site

      This site maps dry-season surface heat across the 20 Local Government Areas of Lagos State, shows where it meets the most people, and tests what goes with cooler places. All data come from one Google Earth Engine export and are recomputed by the repository's own scripts.

      Using the site

      1. Overview gives the plain-language summary, the headline numbers and the findings, each with a verdict.
      2. Map: switch indicators at the top left; click an LGA, or search for it, to open it.
      3. Change since 1985 shows how the heat island grew over four decades; the map's Heat island over time layer plays through the periods and can swipe between then and now.
      4. What explains it compares vegetation and built-up density, and checks Landsat against MODIS.
      5. Statistics is the full audit; Rankings orders the LGAs and compares two side by side.
      6. Share copies a link to exactly this view or prints it; Data downloads everything, including a map image.
